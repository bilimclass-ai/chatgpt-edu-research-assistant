# Question, discipline and evidence adaptation

Select a framework for the research question, not the person's faculty title. PCC suits scoping/concept/context; PICOS suits comparative intervention effectiveness; SPIDER may help qualitative experiences; CIMO supports context/mechanism/implementation; a historical or doctrinal review may need a custom chronology/jurisdiction/concept framework. State the rationale; do not force PICO on all scholarship.

Formal systematic/scoping workflows require explicit criteria, reproducible searches, documented screening and review-specific reporting. Identify which decisions need human adjudication. Reporting checklists are not study quality instruments. Do not claim protocol registration, independent reviewers or completed risk-of-bias appraisal unless actually performed. Meta-analysis requires an explicit request or agreed scope, compatible effect data and defensible methods; never pool studies merely because numbers are available.

| Field or question | Source types and additional extraction | Critical checks |
|---|---|---|
| Clinical/health | Field databases such as MEDLINE/PubMed, trials/registers, reviews; P/I/C/O, dose, follow-up, adverse events, absolute/relative effect and CI | Study design appropriate appraisal; protocol/report discrepancies; attrition; harms; no personal medical advice from the matrix |
| Education/psychology/social sciences | ERIC/PsycINFO or available alternatives; measures, reliability, construct validity, classroom clustering, culture/language, comparator | Perception ≠ learning; cross-sectional association ≠ causal effect; questionnaire validation ≠ intervention effectiveness |
| Computing/AI | ACM/IEEE, conferences, journals and labeled preprints; task, dataset/version, split, benchmark, baselines, compute, code | Leakage, tuning on test sets, reproducibility, fair compute comparison; conference standing may matter more than journal h |
| Engineering/materials/natural sciences | Discipline indexes, journals, standards/patents as separate layers; apparatus/materials, units, calibration, operating conditions, replication | Compare like conditions; laboratory result ≠ deployment; patent claim ≠ independently verified performance |
| Business/economics | EconLit/RePEc/working papers plus journals; identification strategy, assumptions, data vintage, costs and context | Selection/endogeneity, pre-trends where relevant, external validity, working paper versions, publication bias |
| Humanities/history/arts | Books, critical editions, archives, scholarly chapters, primary texts and exhibitions where relevant | Authorship/provenance, translation/edition, interpretive tradition, historiographic disagreement; no h-index exclusion of key books |
| Law/policy | Jurisdiction, authoritative legal text, decisions, effective dates, amendments; academic commentary separately | Current law verified with official sources; precedential authority ≠ citation popularity; no cross-jurisdiction generalization without a bridge |
| Agriculture/environment | Geographic/ecological context, seasons, species, methods, exposure and field/lab conditions | Site/season heterogeneity, units, transferability and non-independence |
| University administration | Evaluations, implementation reports, policy documents, peer-reviewed organizational research; costs, feasibility, equity, service outcomes | Official report ≠ independent causal evidence; vendor claims separate; institutional context matters |
| Interdisciplinary | Explicitly map construct definitions, units, methods and source traditions across fields | Similar labels may represent different constructs; dual code rather than flatten conflicting definitions |

Use field-appropriate appraisal instruments only when current documentation and necessary study data are available. Examples to consider—not automatically apply—include RoB 2 for randomized intervention trials, ROBINS-I for nonrandomized interventions, QUADAS-type instruments for diagnostic accuracy, CASP/JBI approaches for qualitative or other designs, and AMSTAR-type tools for systematic reviews. Verify the current version before claiming formal use; record domain judgments and rationale, not an invented universal quality score. For routine preparatory reviews, a transparent design-specific critique may be more appropriate than a full formal instrument.

Adapt explanation depth by experience, not evidence standards: undergraduate = explain terms and show examples; master's = compare designs and unresolved debates; doctorate/PI = identification, theoretical tensions, counterevidence and novelty; administrator = decision relevance, costs, equity and implementation uncertainty. No level permits fabricated or untraceable evidence.
