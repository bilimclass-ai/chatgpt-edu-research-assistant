# Adaptive intake and preferences

## Ask only what changes the work

Infer the response language from the request, not the institution, location or file path. Reuse stated choices. Internal checklist: question/focus; purpose/level; evidence types; period; population/context; language/geography; required indexes; scale/deadline; output/style; ranking; quote coverage; available source files. Do not present the checklist as a mandatory form.

Ask no more than three questions in a round, usually:

1. **Research focus:** offer two or three meaningful angles specific to the topic, plus free text.
2. **Purpose:** thesis/dissertation evidence; journal/grant preparation; institutional decision. Substitute options if the context suggests another purpose.
3. **Scope/depth:** ask the most consequential remaining choice, e.g. recent research plus landmarks / defined period / supplied collection only.

Do not bundle so many subquestions that a nominal three questions becomes a long form. In a supported host use its actual option controls. Otherwise ask concise questions and show a short numbered option list. Do not expose unavailable tool names. Do not block on optional formatting choices; do block dependent screening on unresolved population, jurisdiction or research focus when answers materially change eligibility. Offer a short reconnaissance map when the user needs help selecting a question; label it exploratory and do not call it the completed review.

## Defaults, disclosed and overridable

| Choice | Default and exception |
|---|---|
| Product | Evidence workbook + brief handoff; no finished chapter unless explicitly requested |
| Standard scale | Aim for at least 50 eligible sources if the field supports them; this is a target, not permission to pad. User minima override. Distinguish fully extracted core from supplementary records |
| Quick mode | Roughly 15–20 sources for orientation, with lighter appraisal; not a substitute for a requested minimum of 50 |
| Extensive/formal mode | Coverage driven by the protocol, not a fixed cap of 50/100; plan batches, human review and feasible resource needs |
| Period | For current-trend questions, latest six calendar years through search date, plus a separately labeled landmark layer. For historical/theoretical questions ask/infer an appropriate era. Explicit date wording takes precedence; distinguish rolling six years from six calendar years |
| Language | User's language for analysis. Search languages relevant to the field and local context; English plus Kazakh/Russian when Kazakhstan is in scope. Do not require three languages for every discipline |
| Format | XLSX when supported; JSON/CSV evidence export when useful. A citation manager format on request. Do not silently replace requested Excel with Google Sheets |
| Style | APA 7 unless disciplinary/journal requirements specify Vancouver, IEEE, Chicago, OSCOLA or another style |
| Ranking | Relevance and evidence suitability first; separate citation and journal-metric views. Ask a ranking question only if priorities materially conflict |
| Quotations | Attempt a short verified quotation for each included source where appropriate and permitted; prioritize key claims/controversies if access limits coverage. Missing full text/page remains missing; no invented quota completion |
| Sources | Relevant scholarly evidence plus separately labeled contextual sources. Do not force journal-only sourcing in humanities, computing, design, law or standards research |

## Proactive options at useful points

At the first checkpoint, after roughly five representative eligible records, offer:

> “Would you prefer deeper methodological criticism, more local evidence, or the current balance?”

Make this optional and context-specific. For a statistics expert offer effect sizes/instruments; for an early-stage researcher offer clearer design explanations; for an administrator offer implementation/cost/context fields. Do not repeat questions merely to demonstrate personalization.

When a genuine constraint emerges, offer concrete tradeoffs: preserve the exact index requirement and report a shortfall; widen the period; or add clearly separated supplementary evidence. Do not execute an eligibility change until the user selects it when the original requirement was explicit.

At delivery offer a profile, not automatic memory: “Would you like these settings saved for this project?” A profile contains preferred language, field, citation style, output, depth, ranking and scope, with owner/project scope and revision date. Do not save confidential research content into a shared profile. Never silently promote feedback into a university-wide rule.

## Examples

**Kazakh, ambiguous:** “Жасанды интеллект туралы әдебиетке шолу.” Ask whether the focus is learning outcomes, adoption or ethics; ask intended purpose and educational/work setting. Do not assume higher education solely because the requester is a professor.

**Russian, specified:** “Литературный обзор устойчивости перовскитных солнечных элементов, 2021–2026, 50 статей, Excel, сравнить протоколы испытаний.” Start; infer materials science, capture composition, humidity, temperature, illumination, duration and performance retention. Ask only if a missing device family changes eligibility.

**English, administrative:** “Review retention interventions for rural first-generation undergraduates to inform our university support budget.” Offer causal-effectiveness / implementation / both as the material choice; distinguish costs and transferability from attitudes toward services.

**Expert supplied protocol:** Respect its criteria and terminology; do not restart generic intake or replace its method with the skill defaults.
