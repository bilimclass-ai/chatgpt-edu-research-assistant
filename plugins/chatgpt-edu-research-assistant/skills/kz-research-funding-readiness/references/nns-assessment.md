# NNS assessment, ranking and role boundaries

**Edition-specific baseline:** NNS-BASE, supplied Order 487 text incorporating 607/2024. Later amendment V2600039378 has been identified but not reconciled. The following is usable for labeled practice or a case where this edition is established as applicable. Do not present it as verified current September 2026 procedure. Read `source-map.md` first.

## Keep the assessment stages separate

Formal admission → scientific GNTE → unscored assessment of funding amount → NNS assessment → combined ranking and route-specific decision → any admissible appeal and implementation → award/contract.

Under baseline §§32,39–40, NNS sees the abstract, GNTE conclusion without its numeric score, budget justification, duplication information and PI's prior funded/completed projects. This makes the abstract and evidenced track record consequential. For a mock NNS review, assess these materials before looking at a hypothetical GNTE total; do not let a high scientific score substitute for NNS evidence.

## NNS scoring: exact baseline scale

Appendices 1–4 assess ordinary grants, PCF, noncompetitive classified-defence programmes and institutional fundamental research respectively. Each declares a 15-point maximum, with three criteria scored 1–5 by a member. Appendix numbers refer to **Order 487**, not Order 517 or funding Order 563.

| Route / appendix | Criterion 1 | Criterion 2 | Criterion 3 |
|---|---|---|---|
| Ordinary grant / 1, PDF pp.28–31 | Contribution to scientific/technical/personnel capacity and competitiveness | Prior groundwork and realistic delivery, including PI workload and past grant leadership | Practical significance, commercialization or other application for Kazakhstan's development, including prior-project effects |
| PCF / 2, pp.32–35 | Plan and budget match the scientific task's difficulty and direct results | Scientific groundwork, material base and qualified team | PI's scientific level and record of organising successful research delivery |
| Noncompetitive classified-defence programme / 3, pp.35–39 | Use the actual appendix and permitted review context; do not infer ordinary open-competition treatment | Read applicable rubric | Read applicable rubric |
| Listed institutional fundamental research / 4, pp.39–42 | Use the actual appendix and institutional route; do not impose grant ranking thresholds | Read applicable rubric | Read applicable rubric |

For ordinary grant/PCF criteria, 1=low, 2=below average, 3=average, 4=above average, 5=high. There is no generic zero in these three-item baseline rubrics. Explain each provisional score using exact evidence; unknown is not automatically zero. Average valid member scores per criterion, then sum the criterion means under §39; respect recusals and verify rounding rather than inventing it.

Commercialization appendix 10 (pp.59–61) has six criteria and a 30-point maximum: result-to-project fit, quality of prior scientific work, planned IP-rights transfer transactions, economic efficiency, co-financing level, and market geography. First four use 1–5. Co-financing is contribution divided by **requested grant**, not total project cost:

| Baseline stated ratio | Points |
|---|---:|
| 100% and above | 5 |
| 51–99% | 4 |
| 31–50% | 3 |
| 21–30% | 2 |
| 11–20% | 1 |
| 10% | 0 |

Do not invent treatment for below 10% or fractional gaps (e.g. 20.5%) or a rounding rule: the displayed bands do not resolve them. Obtain the applicable call/current interpretation. Market geography gives 5 for foreign markets, 4 for one foreign country, 3 for all Kazakhstan, 2 for a Kazakhstan region, 0 if undefined. Do not invent a 1-point band or award foreign-market points without a supported route-to-market plan. A scoring band is not, by itself, the eligibility minimum.

## Baseline ranking and budget risks

- §40 combines GNTE and NNS scores; ordinary grants below a combined 30 are excluded (defence exception). This is distinct from the scientific GNTE threshold and from the eventual budget-driven funding line.
- Ordinary-grant funding goes to the highest-ranked projects within the direction's available budget; tied totals are ranked by increasing selected funding amount. A total above 30 does not guarantee an award.
- PCF ranking is separate for each technical task; the highest total in that task is selected in the baseline text. Do not pool unrelated tasks.
- Special institutional fundamental research and noncompetitive classified-defence routes use their specified voting rules instead of blindly applying ordinary-grant ranking. The baseline additionally excludes defence-direction applications with NNS score below 8 under §39.
- §40 chooses the funding option receiving the most member support; a tie between options selects the smaller. For projects of at most 12 months, the amount established by GNTE is used.
- Ordinary research §40 contains the wording 'на 25 % ... ниже запрошенной' with an institutional exception; commercialization §42 expressly says a cut **more than** 25%. Preserve the distinction and obtain the current provision/interpretation for boundary cases. Do not code one universal ≥25% rejection rule.
- Reconcile all these rules with V2600039378 before a live ranking or rejection forecast. Do not claim the combined maximum or cutoff remains unchanged just because one old rubric is available.

## Membership, ethics and travel

ROSTER-BASE is Order 94 of 6 March 2025, not May 2025. Online ROSTER-238 replaces its commercialization section from its applicable date. Membership can change further; use the current list and meeting record for a specific matter. Do not reproduce a permanent personal directory, predict votes from identities, contact members for favourable treatment or assume affiliation proves a violation.

NNS-BASE appendix 12 covers objective, independent, fair and confidential assessment. Preserve evidence of a potential conflict and verify the precise applicable rule and relationship before asserting misconduct. Baseline §31 has differentiated quorum rules, so do not import appeal-commission quorum into an NNS complaint.

NNS-BASE §51 assigns council-activity travel costs to NCSTE. TRAVEL (board decision 1 of 20 January 2025) concerns council members attending meetings and experts conducting monitoring. Its allowances are not general project travel caps. Use applicant budget sources for research travel. Appeals are governed by Order 423 with amendment 169, not by this membership or reimbursement document.
